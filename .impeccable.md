## Design Context

### Users
Mixed audience — the proposal link gets passed around within the client organisation. Recipients include:
- **IT managers / technical buyers**: Evaluating security features and compliance coverage
- **Procurement officers**: Comparing on price, SLAs, and vendor credibility
- **C-level decision-makers (CEO/CFO)**: Making the final approval call

All of them are enterprise professionals in the MENA region (Egypt, KSA, UAE). They are accustomed to polished vendor documents (PDFs, Word templates) and have high expectations for professional presentation. They view this on whatever device is handy — desktop at the office, phone on the go.

### Brand Personality
**Three words: secure, technical, enterprise**

WorldPosta positions itself as a credible regional alternative to Microsoft/Google — the local expert who understands NCA, NESA, and regional compliance in ways Western providers don't. The tone should feel like a premium vendor briefing, not a startup pitch.

**Emotional goals:**
- **Trust first**: The reader should feel they're dealing with a serious, established organisation — not a startup dressed up in dark mode
- **Excitement second**: The deal should feel compelling — the pricing, the guarantees, the regional advantages should feel like a genuine opportunity
- The overall feel is: *the proposal you'd be proud to forward to your CFO*

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark — MENA enterprise clients reviewing vendor proposals often do so in office environments; dark conveys seriousness and technical authority
- **Tone**: Refined, substantial, confident — not loud, not flashy, not startup-pitch
- **Anti-references**:
  - No startup pitch deck aesthetics (gradient text, blur blobs, hero metric stat cards, neon accents)
  - No consumer app patterns (gamification, emoji icons as primary UI, bouncy animations)
  - Should NOT look like "an AI generated this" — that would undermine trust
- **References to aim for**: Think the print quality of a McKinsey deck, the precision of a Bloomberg terminal, or the authority of an enterprise software contract — adapted for web

### Design Principles
1. **Authority through restraint** — Every decorative element must earn its place. Remove anything that signals "template" or "generated"
2. **Hierarchy by weight, not color** — Use typographic scale and spacing to guide the eye, not gradient text or glowing accents
3. **Trust at every scroll** — Credibility signals (certifications, SLAs, contact info) should feel substantial and legible, not badge-ified
4. **The proposal passes the forward test** — If a CFO screenshots a section and sends it to a board member, it should look professional in that context
5. **The signature moment is sacred** — The acceptance flow (sign section) is the highest-stakes UX moment. It must feel deliberate, reassuring, and serious — not an afterthought
